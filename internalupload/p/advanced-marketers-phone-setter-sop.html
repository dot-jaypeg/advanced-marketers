1. Role & Mission

The Phone Setter is the voice of our clients' businesses (primarily plumbing companies). You answer every inbound call, chase every lead that hasn't answered, and follow up until every opportunity is either booked, sold, or clearly dead. Your job is simple to describe and hard to do well: turn leads into booked appointments and sales as fast as possible, and tell the team what the leads are telling you.

Your two customers

  • The client (business owner): you keep their calendar full, their pipeline updated, and them informed daily.
  • The marketing team: your notes and lead-quality flags are how we optimize campaigns quickly. You are our eyes and ears — if the leads are bad, we need to know the same day, not at month-end.

Success in this role means

  • Every lead is contacted fast — via Call Connect, in under 3 minutes. No exceptions during shift.
  • Every opportunity sits in the correct pipeline stage with notes and a dollar value.
  • Every sold job gets a review request and a referral ask. Every proposal gets relentless, polite follow-up.
  • The business owner knows their schedule every day. Marketing knows lead quality every day.

Mindset (from the AM Sales Framework): You are not the show — they are. Let the process do the work. Skip the fluff. Assume nothing on the lead form is 100% accurate — validate everything. Don't pitch; ask smart questions and stay curious. Avoid jargon: confuse the customer, lose the sale.

3. The Standard Pipeline

Every client account uses this stage flow in GHL (mirror it into the client CRM where one exists). Move opportunities the moment status changes — the faster leads move through the pipeline, the better. An opportunity may never skip stages without a note explaining why.

StageDefinitionYour action / exit rule
1. New LeadLead just came in (Meta, Google, LSA, inbound call).Call Connect within 3 minutes. Set the opportunity value. Move to Contacted after first live conversation.
2. ContactedLive conversation happened; not yet booked.Qualify, book the appointment. If no answer, run the outreach cadence (Section 5).
3. Appointment BookedEstimate/service visit is on the calendar.Confirm the tech/company is dispatched (5.5). Confirm customer day before + morning of. No-show? Rebook same day and note it.
4. Proposal / Estimate SentClient gave a price or sent a proposal.Follow-up cadence: day 1, 3, 5, 7, then weekly. Log every touch.
5. Sold / WonCustomer accepted; job scheduled or done.Enter final job value. Within 24h of job completion: review request + happy-check referral call (5.6).
6. Review + Referral RequestedReview ask sent and referral call made.One reminder 3 days later if no review. Log any referral names as new leads. Then close.
7. Lost / NurtureSaid no, unresponsive after full cadence, or bad fit.Tag the reason (price / unresponsive / bad-fit / competitor / spam). This tag is gold for marketing.

Non-negotiables for every opportunity

  • Dollar value filled in. Estimate it from the job type if unknown (client's average ticket). Update it when a real quote exists.
  • Notes after every touch. Who you spoke to, what they need, objections, next step + date. The more notes, the better.
  • Lead source visible. Never strip campaign/source tags — marketing needs them for attribution.
  • Next action scheduled. No opportunity sits without a task or follow-up date on it.

5. Core Workflows

5.1 Inbound calls (answer the phone!)

  • Answer within 3 rings, live, every time. A missed call is lost revenue — plumbing customers call the next company on the list.
  • Missed anyway? Call back within 3 minutes and send the missed-call text (Section 6) immediately.
  • During the call: create/update the contact in GHL, qualify with the discovery questions, and book while on the phone. Do not hang up to "check the calendar" — book live.
  • After the call: notes, value, stage move, confirmation text. 2 minutes max, then back to the queue.

5.2 Speed-to-lead: new Meta/form leads

  • Call Connect within 3 minutes of the lead arriving. Contact rates fall off a cliff after that.
  • No answer on first call? Send First-Touch Text immediately. Save the voicemail for the LAST call of the day (see cadence below).
  • Leads under 24 hours old are priority one: they get three touches that first day, spread across morning / midday / end of day.

5.3 No-answer outreach cadence (leads that haven't answered)

Every call attempt is paired with a text. Spread attempts across different times of day. Rotate to a different phone number when the schedule says so — a number they haven't ignored yet gets picked up. Leave a voicemail only on the final call of each calling day.

DayCallsHow to run it
Day 13Morning, midday, end of day — each call + text. Final call of the day: switch to a different phone number, and leave the voicemail after it.
Day 22Two different times of day, call + text each. Voicemail only after the last call.
Day 31One call + text at a time of day you haven't tried yet.
Day 52Two calls + texts ("last check-in" tone). Rotate number on at least one.
Day 73Three calls at different times, rotating numbers + breakup text. Voicemail after the final call. Still nothing → Lost/Nurture, tag "unresponsive".

Total: 11 call attempts across 5 calling days. Never exceed 3 calls to one lead in a day. Never more than 1 unsolicited text thread-start per day per lead. Stop immediately on any opt-out ("STOP", "don't call") — tag DND in GHL.

5.4 Proposal / estimate follow-up

  • Cadence after a proposal is sent: Day 1 (did you get it / any questions), Day 3 (call), Day 5 (text value angle), Day 7 (call + decision ask), then weekly for 4 weeks.
  • Every follow-up must add something: answer a question, restate value, share a review or photo of similar jobs — never just "checking in" twice in a row.
  • After the full cadence with no decision → Lost/Nurture, tagged "went-dark-after-proposal", with a note on last known objection.

5.5 Booking appointments, calendar & tech dispatch

  • Book directly on the client's calendar, respecting their service hours and travel zones. Use alternate-choice closes: "Tomorrow at 10 or Thursday at 2?"
  • Make sure the tech actually goes out. When you book a job: (1) notify the tech/company that the appointment exists, (2) get confirmation they're aware and will be there, (3) re-verify the morning of. If they can't make it, reschedule the customer BEFORE the window is missed.
  • Confirmation text to the customer goes out immediately; reminder day before and morning of.
  • Any change (cancel, reschedule, emergency squeeze-in) → update calendar AND tell the owner in Slack right away. The owner should never be surprised by their own calendar — and a customer should never wait on a tech who was never coming.

5.6 After the sale: review + referral (every sold job)

  • Within 24 hours of job completion, send the review request text (Section 6) with the client's Google review link.
  • Referral call: call the customer, check how everything went (this also catches problems before they become bad reviews), then ask for a referral — script in 6.8.
  • No review after 3 days? One friendly reminder. Log "review requested / review received / referral asked / referral received" on the contact. Any referral name goes in as a new lead, source-tagged "referral — [customer name]".

6. Scripts & Templates (AM Sales Framework)

These follow the Advanced Marketers sales script structure — Opening & agenda → Discovery → Recap & solution → Close → Objection handling — adapted for homeowner calls for our plumbing clients. Replace [Client Company] with the client's business name. Say it naturally; don't read robotically.

6.1 Inbound call opening + agenda

"Thanks for calling [Client Company], this is [Name] — how can I help you today?"

(Let them explain. Do not interrupt — this is where you collect the problem, urgency, and their own words.)

"Got it — sorry you're dealing with that. Let me ask you a couple quick questions so we can get the right tech out to you, is that okay?" (Get a clear yes.)

6.2 Discovery (uncover the story)

  • The problem: "Walk me through what's going on — what are you seeing?"
  • Duration/urgency: "How long has this been happening? Is it getting worse / is water actively leaking right now?"
  • Property: "Is this a house or a business? Do you own the property?" (Renters may need landlord approval.)
  • Location: "What's the address / what area are you in?" (Confirm service area before booking.)
  • History: "Have you had anyone look at it yet? What did they say?" (Surfaces price shopping and competitor quotes.)
  • Decision: "If our tech can fix it same-visit, are you good to move forward, or does anyone else weigh in?"

While they talk, fill in: job type, urgency, estimated value (use client's average ticket for the job type), and their exact words — marketing uses this language in ads.

6.3 Recap + book (the close)

"Just to recap — you've got [their problem, their words], it's been going on [time], and you want it handled [urgency]. Right?" (Wait for agreement — this is the goal close.)

"Perfect. We can have a technician out [day/time window] or [alternate day/time] — which works better for you?"

After booking: "You're all set for [day/time]. You'll get a text confirmation right now, and a reminder the day before. The tech will call when they're on the way. Anything else I can help with?"

6.4 Outbound call to an unanswered Meta lead

"Hi, is this [First Name]? This is [Name] with [Client Company] — you reached out to us on Facebook about [service, e.g., a water heater issue]. Is it okay if I ask you a couple quick questions so we can get this handled for you?" (Get the yes, then go straight into Discovery 6.2 and book.)

(If "how did you get my number?") "You filled out a quick form on our ad — totally quick process. You were asking about [service]. Is that still something you need handled?"

6.5 Voicemail (final call of the day only — under 20 seconds)

"Hi [First Name], this is [Name] with [Client Company] — you reached out about [service]. I've texted you so you have my number. Call or text back whenever's easy. Talk soon!"

6.7 Objection handling (probe → acknowledge → refocus → close)

ObjectionHow to handle it
"How much is it?" (price shopping)Probe: "Totally fair — so I don't guess wrong, it depends on [factors]. That's exactly why we do an estimate visit [free / for $X — use the client's policy]." Refocus on the visit: "Let's get eyes on it so you get a real number, not a phone guess — [day] or [day]?"
"That's too expensive"Probe: "Help me understand — when you say too expensive, what are you comparing it to?" Acknowledge, then show value: licensed techs, warranty, done-right-once. Close: "Is that difference really worth risking a redo?"
"I need to talk to my spouse"Acknowledge and align: "Of course." Then keep control: "What questions do you think they'll have? … Let's pencil [day] — if it doesn't work after you talk, one text and we move it."
"I already called someone else""Smart to compare. Most folks who get a second opinion from us are glad they did — the estimate visit is [free / $X, credited toward the job — per client policy], so you've lost nothing. Want me to set it up?"
Apathetic / cold ("just looking")Probe for the real situation, acknowledge, tell them what you'd like to do and why (estimate visit, no pressure), and ask for agreement.

Estimate pricing varies by client — check the client profile before quoting "free." If the client charges for estimate visits, lead with the value: "$X, and it's credited toward the job if you move forward."

6.8 Referral call (after every sold job)

"Hi [First Name], it's [Name] with [Client Company] — I'm not calling to sell you anything, I just wanted to make sure everything went great with [the job]. How'd [Tech Name] do?"

(Listen. If there's ANY problem — fix it first, escalate to the owner, and do NOT ask for the referral or review on this call.)

(If happy:) "That's great to hear. Quick favor — most of our best customers come from folks like you. Is there a neighbor, friend, or family member dealing with any plumbing stuff we could help out? We'll take great care of anyone you send."

(If they give a name:) "Perfect — want me to reach out to them directly, or would you rather pass along our number? Either way, we'll mention you sent them."

7. Notes, Lead Value & Data Hygiene

If it isn't in GHL (and the client's CRM, where one exists), it didn't happen. Marketing decisions, owner schedules, and your own performance reviews all run off this data.

FieldStandard
Opportunity valueAlways filled. Real quote if it exists; otherwise the client's average ticket for that job type. Update on change.
NotesAfter every touch: who/what/objection/next step + date. Use the customer's own words for the problem.
TagsLead quality (good-lead / bad-lead + reason), DND/opt-out, lost reason, review-requested, referral-asked.
Lead quality flagMark every worked lead good or bad SAME DAY. Bad = wrong service, out of area, no intent, fake number, price-only shopper. This is what lets marketing kill bad campaigns fast.
CRM syncWhere a client runs their own CRM, mirror stage + notes per that client's playbook. Log any sync friction in Slack same day.

12. Hard Rules

  • Never promise a price. Ranges the client approved only; otherwise "the tech will quote it on site."
  • Never ignore an opt-out. "Stop," "don't call," "remove me" = DND tag immediately, forever.
  • Never leave a worked lead without notes, a stage, and a next step.
  • Never let the owner find out about a schedule change from the customer — and never let a customer wait on a tech nobody dispatched.
  • Never quote "free estimate" without checking the client's estimate-visit policy.
  • Always book while on the phone. "I'll call you back to schedule" is a lost job.
  • Always clear messages and missed calls before starting new outbound work.
  • Always flag lead quality the same day. A bad campaign running one extra week is real money wasted.
