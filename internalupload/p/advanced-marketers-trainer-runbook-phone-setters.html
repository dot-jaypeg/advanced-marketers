1. Tonight: Prep Checklist (before trainees walk in)

  • 4 × GHL logins created, assigned to a training sub-account (never a live client account on day 1) with test contacts loaded
  • Call Connect tested on each login; headsets working
  • Slack: all 4 invited to #operations + a #setter-training channel; trainer posts welcome message tonight
  • Send each trainee: Phone Setter SOP v1.1 + Setter Call Script (All Trades) — tell them to read the SOP before arrival (they won't all do it; plan for it)
  • Pick 10 call recordings: 5 great, 5 bad (missed discovery, no close, long rambling). Queue them up
  • Print or share: client one-pagers for each account they'll support — services, service area, average tickets, estimate-visit policy (free or $X), techs' names
  • Print 4 copies of the scoring rubric (Section 5)
  • Book the room / Zoom for the full day; block your own calendar — the trainer does not take other meetings on Day 1
  • Assign pairs for role-play (4 people = 2 pairs, rotate partners after lunch)

2. How to Train (Principles)

  • Talk less, drill more. Max 30 minutes of you presenting before they DO something. The script is learned by mouth, not by eyes.
  • Model → Together → Alone. You demo it, you run it with them, they run it with each other. Every skill, same order.
  • Coach the framework, not the words. Grade opening / discovery / recap-close / objection structure. Their phrasing can flex; the sequence can't.
  • Praise publicly, correct specifically. One fix at a time per person. "Do X instead of Y next rep" beats a list of five sins.
  • Everything they'll do live, they do in training first: notes, opportunity values, stage moves, Slack EOD — from day one, even on fake leads.

4. Role-Play Drill Bank

Trainer or partner plays the customer. Keep reps short (3–6 min) and repeat until smooth. Escalate difficulty only when the previous drill is passed.

#ScenarioCustomer behavior (for the person playing customer)
1Inbound: plumbing, water heater not heatingCooperative. Answers everything. Trainee should reach recap + book in under 6 min.
2Inbound: junk removal, garage cleanoutImpatient — "just give me a price." Trainee gives range + on-site confirm + books soonest window.
3Inbound: HVAC, AC blowing warm, 14-year-old unitChatty, wanders off topic. Trainee must politely steer back with questions and flag unit age.
4Outbound: Meta roofing lead, didn't answer for 2 daysSuspicious: "how did you get my number?" Then mentions hail last month. Trainee must catch the insurance flag and sell the free inspection.
5Inbound: flooring, "just pricing" 3 bedroomsNon-committal. Trainee sells the free in-home measure + samples, creates urgency from their "before the holidays" comment.
6Objection gauntlet (days 2–4)Customer throws all 5 objections in one call: price question, too expensive, spouse, already called someone, just looking. Pass = handles each with Probe → Acknowledge → Refocus → Close.
7Emergency (days 2–4): burst pipe, panickingTalking fast, scared. Trainee must calm, get address + shutoff status, book same-day, and know to escalate to owner if no slot.
8Referral call (days 3–5): job completed yesterdayHappy customer. Trainee runs happy-check → review ask → referral ask. Variant: customer has a complaint — trainee must NOT ask for review, must escalate.

5. Scoring Rubric (per role-play / call review)

Score each area 1–5. Same rubric used later for weekly live-call QA, so trainees learn the bar from day one.

Area5 looks like1 looks like
Opening & agendaWarm, confident, gets explicit yes to questionsRobotic read, skips permission, pitches immediately
DiscoveryAll 6 universal Qs + trade money question; lets customer talk; uses their wordsInterrogation or skips to price; misses urgency/insurance/age flags
Recap + closeRecaps in customer's words, gets the yes, alternate-choice books on the callNo recap; "I'll call you back to schedule"; asks "when works for you?" open-ended
Objection handlingProbe → Acknowledge → Refocus → Close; stays calmArgues, caves instantly, or freezes
Tone & controlCurious, unhurried, steers politelyTalks over customer, jargon, sounds scared of silence
Paper trail (GHL)Notes/value/stage/next task complete within 2 minMissing or vague notes, no value, no next step

Passing bar during week 1: average ≥ 3.5 with no area below 3. Log scores per trainee per day — you'll need the trendline for the day-7 decision.

7. Pass/Fail Gates & Strugglers

  • Day 2 gate: can't run opening + discovery from memory → extra 1:1 drilling that afternoon; partner them with your strongest trainee.
  • Day 4 gate: fails the objection gauntlet twice → they do NOT go live on day 5. Give them day 5 as a repeat drill day; retest morning of day 6.
  • Day 7 gate: below bar on the rubric trendline → one extension week with daily 1:1 coaching, clear written expectations, and a named metric to fix. No improvement by day 14 → not a fit; better for everyone to know early.
  • Watch for the two failure patterns: the reader (glued to the script, can't listen) → force bullet-point-only reps; and the winger (charming, skips discovery) → grade them hard on the rubric, show them the missed money questions.
Never let a struggling trainee practice on live leads. Real leads cost the client real money. Training accounts are free.
