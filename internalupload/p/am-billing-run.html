The Billing Run

How we charge clients. Base plan plus 10% of what they spent on ads. Runs every weekday morning.

Owner: Victor Lemus.

You are the only person who charges cards. If you don't run this, the money doesn't come in — there is no automation behind you and no one else checking.

Where everything lives

One private space, 🔒 Accounting. Two lists inside it.

The other list is 📒 Client Billing Records — one card per client, 90 of them. That's where Billing Day, Base Amount and Billing Type live.

Every weekday morning

1

Open today's queue

Go to 📒 Client Billing Records and sort by Billing Day. Today's date is today's list. Usually two to five clients.

2

Check Billing Type before you calculate anything

This decides whether you charge at all. Get it wrong and you double-bill someone.

TypeWhat you do
RecurringCharge normally — go to step 3
UpsellCharge normally — a real second line on the same client
One-timeDo not charge. Already paid once. Skip it.
PrepaidBase is already paid. Charge only the 10% of ad spend.
Autopay — verify onlyDo not charge — but do not skip. See step 7.

A client can have two cards, and that's correct. Major League Plumbing: ads $2,500 on the 2nd, SEO $1,500 on the 18th. Not duplicates. Don't merge them, don't skip the second one.

3

Set the base amount

The Base Amount field on the client's card is the truth. This table is just so you know what it should look like.

PackageStandardFirst 2 months
Foundation$3,500$2,500
Accelerator$5,000$4,000
Empire$6,500$5,500

It's $1,000 off for the first two months on any tier — same discount whether they prepay both months or pay monthly. Upsells are usually $1,500. Website-only is $499.

Month 3 is where we leak money. Check the Intro Ends date every single time. If that date has passed and they're still at the intro rate, they should be at standard. Missing it costs $1,000 a month until somebody notices — and nobody notices.

4

Pull Google Ads spend — last 30 days

Rolling 30 days from today. Not the calendar month.

Pull the ACCOUNT total, not a campaign report. Campaign views hide removed and paused campaigns by default. A campaign that spent $4,000 and was then deleted is invisible in a campaign report but present in the account total. We bill on what was spent.

And never use the invoice. Google charges on a billing threshold, so the amount charged in a period is not the amount spent in it.

5

Check LSA — is it already counted?

This is the step that can overcharge a client. Read it every time.

A
There's a Local Services campaign inside Google Ads → it's already in your total. Add nothing.
B
There isn't one → it's a separate account. Go to ads.google.com/localservices, set Last 30 days, screenshot it, add it on top.
C
You're not sure → stop and ask the Growth Specialist. Do not guess.

An undercharge costs us one month. An overcharge costs us the account.

Google started moving LSA into Google Ads in August 2026 — US home services first, which is most of our book. Clients are moving from B to A as they migrate. Check every time. Last month's answer expires.

6

Add Meta and Yelp, then do the math

Meta — account-level spend, last 30 days. Account level, not campaign level, same reason as Google.

Yelp — log in at biz.yelp.com, open the client's business, and find their ad spend for the last 30 days. Logins are in Links & Logins. Every client is a separate Yelp login — there is no single dashboard that shows all of them, so you have to go in one at a time.

Same rule as Google: spend, not the statement. Yelp bills on its own cycle, so the amount on the invoice is not the amount spent in your 30-day window. Take the spend figure.

If the client doesn't run Yelp, it's $0 and you move on. If you can't tell whether they run Yelp, ask the Growth Specialist — same as LSA. Don't assume $0 because you couldn't find a login.

base $3,500.00 Google Ads $1,400 Meta $520 Yelp $0 LSA $127 ──────────────────────── ad spend $2,047 × 10% = $204.70 ════════════════════════ CHARGE $3,704.70

Sanity check before you hit charge. Does it look like last month? Forbes Fencing lands near $3,700 every month. A swing of hundreds means stop and find out why first. A wrong charge is much harder to undo than a late one.

Charge the saved card on the contact record in GoHighLevel — Contact → Payments. It's already vaulted, so you're clicking, never typing. Primary merchant first. PayBright only after a confirmed decline, once per client per cycle.

7

Autopay clients — verify, don't charge

Some smaller accounts run on automatic charges in PayBright. Their card says Autopay — verify only.

Don't charge them — they've already been charged, and charging again bills them twice. But don't skip them either. On their billing day, open PayBright and confirm the charge actually went through.

If the autopay failed, it's a decline. Work it like any other decline.

This is the one that goes unnoticed. A $199 autopay that quietly fails in September is still failing in March — $1,200 gone on a client nobody's watching, because nobody misses a small number the way they'd miss $3,500. Nothing appears on a screen when an autopay fails. The only thing between that and lost revenue is you opening PayBright and looking.

8

Close out the day

Charged in full → nothing more to make. It's logged by the charge itself.

Declined or partial → make a task. Next section shows exactly where it goes.

Then one instant payout on the whole batch, not one per client.

9

Finish the Daily Billing Run card — comment first, then complete

When the day's queue is charged, you mark 🗓️ Daily Billing Run complete. It comes back on its own tomorrow morning — you don't create a new one.

Leave a comment before you complete it. Every day, even a quiet one. A card closed with nothing in it says the same thing as a card nobody touched, and there's no other record that the day was worked.

Four lines is enough. Copy this:

Clients due: 4 Charged: 3 — $11,412.30 Declined: 1 — Roof City $3,785 (task made) Autopay checked: 1 — Aspen $499 went through

This is what makes month close possible. At the end of the month you read back through these comments and the three numbers are already there. Without them, month close means reconstructing thirty days from memory.

If a client on today's list didn't get charged for any reason — you couldn't reach their ad account, you weren't sure about LSA, anything — say so in the comment and carry them to tomorrow. An unexplained skip is how a client goes three months without being billed.

When a card declines

The task does NOT go in the Accounting space. It goes in the client's own folder in 🚀 AM Delivery 2.0, where their Growth Specialist already works. They should never have to go into another space to find out their client owes money.

🚀 AM Delivery 2.0 └── 📁 Roof City Professionals ← the client's folder └── 📋 Accounting ← parent task └── ☑ Declined — $3,785 ← subtask, due TODAY ✕ NOT in 🔒 Accounting. That space is yours, not theirs.

Put the amount in the title so the list adds up at a glance without opening anything. Copy the wording from 📋 TEMPLATE — Declined in the Billing Desk.

Day 0

Retry on the primary merchant once. Still declining → retry on PayBright. Once. That's the whole backup rule.

Confirmed decline → text and email the client from GHL. Same day, not tomorrow.

Tag the client's Growth Manager in the task right now. They get it first, on purpose.

Days 1–3

You check in with the Growth Specialist every day and write the status in the task each time.

The Growth Manager reaches out to the client directly — they have the relationship, and most of these end here. A bounced card is usually an expired card or a bank flag, not a client refusing to pay.

Day 4

No update? Erika Jaramillo takes over. She owns it from here and works it every day for a week — on her daily list alongside the Nuve calls, not a one-off attempt.

One channel is not trying. Rotate every day: phone call, voicemail, SMS, DM from the Advanced Marketers social accounts, and a call from a different number. Someone who ignores one number will pick up another. Someone who never answers the phone will reply to a DM.

Log every attempt: what channel, what time, what happened.

Day 5

Erika calls again and sends the written pause notice by email, so there's a record. Note both in the task.

Day 7

Still unrecovered after a week → tag @Tim Rodriguez. He owns the pause-or-continue decision.

It stops being a billing problem and becomes a retention one. Everything Tim needs should already be in the comments — every call, every message, every date.

Never hang up without someone live. If the client asks about their ad account, transfer live — first Paul or Tim, second their Growth Specialist. If nobody is reachable, book a confirmed calendar time with a named person while the client is still on the phone. "Someone will reach out" does not close the call.

Closing a decline task

RECOVERED → note what finally worked, add ✅ to the front of the title, complete it.

PARTIAL PAYMENT → log what came in, set Balance Due to the remainder, and leave it open until Balance Due is zero.

WRITTEN OFF → note Tim's decision and the date, add ❌ to the front of the title, complete it.

That's the whole reporting system. At month end the ✅ titles are recovered revenue and the ❌ titles are lost revenue, and you can add them up by reading the list.

A task closed with no notes tells the next person nothing. In three weeks nobody remembers which number answered or what was promised. Closing silently is worse than not doing it — because now it looks handled.

When a client cancels

Two things are not your call.

Whether they've cancelled. It counts when it's in writing, on a recorded call, or it comes from Tim, Paul or Chris. "I heard they're leaving" is not a cancellation — keep billing normally until someone confirms it.

Whether we take the final charge. That's Tim's or Chris's decision, not yours. You flag it, they answer.

Everything below happens the same day you're told.

1

Take them out of the queue

Open their card in 📒 Client Billing Records. Put ❌ in front of the name and set the status to Complete. They stop showing up in your daily queue from tomorrow.

Do not delete the card. It's the record of everything they ever paid us. Completing it means "not billing any more" — deleting it means the history is gone.

2

Kill the recurring charge in BOTH places

Cancel the subscription in GoHighLevel. Then open PayBright and cancel it there too.

Check both even if you only remember setting up one. This is the autopay problem in reverse — a PayBright autopay nobody cancelled keeps charging a client who already left. That's a refund, an angry phone call, and possibly a chargeback, and like every autopay problem it makes no noise. Nobody finds out until the client's bank does.

3

Ask about the final bill — don't answer it yourself

There's almost always ad spend from the last cycle that hasn't been billed yet, and depending on their contract there may be a final charge on top.

Work out the number, then post it in the retention task and tag @Tim Rodriguez:

Final billing — Roof City Professionals Last 30 days ad spend: $2,047 10% owed: $204.70 Base for final period: $3,500 (contract Q for Tim) ──────────────────────────────── Charge or waive?

Do not charge it and do not waive it on your own. Charging a client after they cancelled is a refund-first situation — we give the money back and ask questions later, and it costs us the referral too.

4

Confirm the ads actually stopped

Turning ads off isn't your job. Noticing they didn't is, because we bill 10% of spend — so every dollar spent after a cancellation is a dollar we pay for and can't collect.

This has already cost us. Over $7,000 of one client's ad budget kept spending after they cancelled in writing. If you see spend continuing on a cancelled account, say so in the retention task immediately — don't assume someone else noticed.

5

Log it

Note the cancellation date on their billing card, and put it in that day's Daily Billing Run comment like anything else:

Cancelled: Roof City — confirmed by Tim 9/1. Card ❌ + completed, GHL sub cancelled, PayBright autopay cancelled. Final $3,704.70 flagged to Tim, awaiting decision.

Where the retention side lives: 🚀 AM Delivery 2.0 → 🚨 Retention — Cancel & Save Pipeline → RETENTION & CANCELLATION CLIENTS. Tim owns that list. Your billing notes go in the client's task there so everything about the cancellation sits in one place.

The nevers

  • Never type a card number anywhere. Cards are added once, by Chris, into GHL Payments and PayBright. After that you charge by clicking. Last four and expiry are the only card details that belong in a task.
  • Never bill off the invoice. Bill off spend. The platforms charge on thresholds, so what they charged is not what was spent.
  • Never guess on LSA. Ask the Growth Specialist. An overcharge costs us the account.
  • Never skip an autopay client. You're not charging them — you're confirming they got charged.
  • Never close the daily card without a comment. Four lines. It's the only record the day happened.
  • Never charge a client who has cancelled without Tim or Chris saying so — and never cancel only one merchant.
Questions on any of this — ask Chris. If something in here doesn't match what you're seeing on screen, say so rather than working around it; the process being wrong on paper is a bug worth fixing.
