Cancels & Saves

One document. Save is how you keep a client who wants out. Cancel is what happens when you cannot, including what we charge them to keep their website. Reference is where everything lives and what is still broken.

The first hour 01

The first sixty minutes decide most of these. A client who asks to cancel and hears nothing for a day has already left, they are just waiting for the paperwork.

STOP THE BILLING. Within one hour. You do not need permission for any of this.

The moment a client says the word cancel, three things happen. Nobody has to approve them and nobody has to be asked.

  1. Flag billing. No further charge until Christian rules on it. Money is reversible and it is the thing that turns a cancel into a chargeback.
  2. File the retention card and put one name on it. Inside the hour.
  3. Write to the client confirming the next charge is held and telling them what happens next.

Christian rules on the refund after those three, not before.

Do not pause the ads yet

They asked to cancel. They have not cancelled. If we save them, the ads never should have stopped, and a campaign that gets paused and restarted comes back worse. Learning resets, pacing resets, LSA ranking drops. You would be handing yourself a harder save than the one you started with.

When the ads do get paused

They actually cancelledConfirmed, not threatened. Pause Google, Meta and LSA that day, then run the Cancel tab.
The save has been stuck over 10 daysNo traction, no answer, no decision. Ten days is the line. Pause the spend and tell Christian it is stuck.
Why this rule changed

It used to say pause everything on day one. That was wrong in one direction, because it protected the money by damaging the account we were trying to keep. Holding the billing on day one and holding the ads until it is real gets both.

It is still true that ads left running on a client who genuinely cancelled is how we get refunds. MGS Air asked to cancel and the ads were still running a week later, $4,000 spent. Roofing Pro was billed roughly three weeks past cancel. Down The Line was charged $4,200 after cancelling. Inland Renovation was double-charged. All City Janitorial cancelled and then got staffed with a phone setter two days later. The billing flag on day one is what stops that, and the 10-day line is what stops a stuck save quietly burning spend for a month.

Day 2Plan

Write the save plan on the card

What is actually wrong, what we are changing, who is doing it, and by when. Three lines is enough. A save with no written plan is a hope.

Do not cancel the billing yourself

Ever. You tell the person who handles billing, same day, with the client name, the amount and the last day of service, and you get confirmation back that it is done. Cancelling it yourself is how a client gets charged after they left, and that turns a clean loss into a chargeback and a review.

Paid or free 02

A save is a fixed window where the client stops deciding and starts watching. You are not talking them out of leaving. You are buying a defined stretch of time, agreeing what you will prove inside it, and proving it where they can see.

A save is a promise with a deadline on it

If there is no end date, there is no save. There is a client who is still leaving, just more slowly, while everyone tells each other it is being handled.

Every save is paid or free. Decide first.

Paid proof periodThey keep paying. Billing continues untouched. This is the default and the stronger position, because the client is still buying and we are still delivering. Nothing has to be unwound if it works.
Free monthWe eat a month to buy the time. Billing has to be stopped by hand, which is a task on Victor Lemus, which is a thing that can be missed. Only when the account genuinely earned it.

When a free month is on the table

  • Four or more months with us, and
  • No converted leads in that time. Not "they are unhappy". Not "they yelled". Nothing landed.
  • Never offered on the intake call. Diagnose first. The moment you lead with a free month you have taught the client that complaining is how they get a discount, and you will see them again next quarter.
  • Christian approves it in advance. Free months, credits, price changes and package changes are his call, not yours.

The clock starts when they can see something

Not when they said the word cancel. Not when you got off the call. The window opens the day the client can actually watch the work happen, which usually means the day their CRM access is live and the ads are back on. Pick that date, write it in the card, email it to the client so both sides are counting the same days.

A failed saveOne call, a promise to keep an eye on it, a card that sits in save attempt for four months, a client who quietly stops paying. Nobody can say what was tried.
A real saveThe reason is named. Something concrete changes in week one. The client hears from us twice a week with something new each time. A decision lands inside the window.

Day one 03

Five things happen in the first twenty-four hours. All five in writing. Email, not Slack, not a text, not "I told him on the phone."

  1. Confirm the terms to the client in writing. What the window is, when it starts, when it ends, what we will do inside it. One email, copied onto the card.
  2. Agree the day-30 number with them, out loud, on the first call. Then write it into the card. See The number. This is the part everybody skips.
  3. Confirm the ad status and the next billing date. They asked about money. Answer the money question before they have to ask twice.
  4. Billing hold, if this is a free month. A task on Victor Lemus, dated today, confirming the account will not be charged.
  5. Refund, if one is owed. Also Victor Lemus, also today, and the amount and date get confirmed to the client in writing.
Nothing on a save card is verbal

Every promise you make to that client goes on the card the day you make it. Not because we do not trust you. Because in thirty days somebody will ask what we agreed, and the answer needs to exist somewhere other than your memory.

The number 04

The most important sentence in a save is the client telling you, in their own words, what would make them stay. Until you have that, you are guessing at a target and you will hit something they never cared about.

What not to doDecide internally that twenty leads is a good month, deliver twenty leads, and find out on day 30 that he wanted three booked jobs and does not care about lead count at all.
What to doAsk him on the first call. "Thirty days from now, what has to have happened for you to feel good about this?" Read it back. Put his words in the card.

Rules on the number

  • It comes from them, not from us. You are writing down their standard, not negotiating ours.
  • No multiples, ever. Do not say 3x. Do not say 10x. Do not promise a return you do not control. We control leads, speed and quality of work. We do not control whether he answers his phone.
  • Know their ad spend before you agree to anything. A number that is impossible at their budget is a number you have just promised to miss.
  • If they will not give you one, that is information. A client who cannot say what would make them happy has usually already decided. Raise it with Christian the same day.
  • Write it in the card before the call ends, or it will not get written at all.

The cadence 05

This is the rule that changes retention. Two calls a week, every week, until it resolves.

Who gets two a week

Every client on a free month, automatically. Plus any other save where the manager decides it warrants that cadence. It is not automatic on every retention card.

That is a capacity call, not a softening. Nineteen live cards at twice a week is thirty-eight calls a week on a team already over capacity. Spend the intensity on the accounts that are actually in a proof window.

Every save call reports something that changed since the last one

If you have nothing new to tell them, the problem is not the phone call. The problem is that nobody worked the account this week. Fix that, then call.

Rules for the cadence

  • Two calls, not two touches. A text is not a call. An email is not a call. Texts are what you send after the call.
  • Different days. Both calls on Friday is one call with extra steps.
  • Every attempt gets logged on the card. A save with no notes is treated as untouched, and that is not a technicality, it is how a card ends up sitting for four months.
  • If they do not pick up, rotate numbers, call the business line, call the number on their Google Business Profile, message the business on Instagram. Twenty dials from one number they are screening is one attempt.
  • The cadence does not pause because you are waiting on another department. If you are blocked, the call is where you say so honestly and give them a date.
  • It ends when the window ends, one way or the other. The decision call happens two days before the deadline, never after it. See The decision call.
Why this rule exists

There are accounts sitting in save attempt that have been there for months. Almost none have a note explaining what was tried. Every one of them is money we already earned, still sitting on the table.

What goes on the card after each call is in Every call.

Every call 06

The call is not finished when you hang up. It is finished when the comment is on the card. Three things, every time, no exceptions.

1Outcome

Held, client no-show, or rescheduled

One of those three words. Not "tried him". If he did not show, the word is no-show, because that fact matters later.

2Leads

Leads sent since the last call

The actual count. This is the spine of the whole thing. Thirty days of these comments is the evidence that we did the work, and it is the only defence we have if the client says we did nothing.

3Words

What the client said

Their words, not your summary of their mood. "His guy is slow returning calls" is useful. "Seemed fine" is not.

Two no-shows changes the conversation

A client who will not get on the phone twice inside a proof window has usually already left and has not told you. That is not a reason to chase harder. It is a reason to raise it with Christian before the decision call, so nobody is surprised.

It also protects you. "We called, he did not show, here are the dates" is a fact. "We were in touch" is an argument you will lose.

The decision call 07

Every save ends on a call where somebody says the word. Renew or release. It happens two days before the deadline, never after it.

-2Days

Two days before the window closes

Growth owner and Christian, together. Before the deadline, so there is room to act on whatever gets decided. After the deadline you are not deciding anything, you are reacting to a client who already made the call for you.

What you bring to it

  • The number they gave you on day one, in their words.
  • What actually happened against it. Leads sent, across the whole window, from your call comments.
  • Call record. How many held, how many no-shows.
  • Your read. Do they stay, and do we want them to. Both halves are real questions.
Release is a legitimate outcome

Some accounts should leave. An account that cannot be made to work at their budget, or a client who will not pick up the phone, is costing the team more than it pays. Saying so at the decision call is the job. Dragging it another sixty days is not.

The save card 08

The save gets built as its own card inside the client's own folder, so the people doing the work see it where they already are. The retention card is the record that they asked to leave. The save card is the work.

Header, set at creation

  • Client and growth owner. One owner. Never two.
  • Save date and proof window. Start and end, both real dates.
  • Cadence. Two a week or one a week. Manager decides.
  • Paid or free.
  • Refund owed. Yes or no, and the amount.
  • What success looks like at day 30. Required. No blanks. This is the number from The number.

It starts when the save fails 01

Nothing below happens while you are still trying to keep them. The switch is the retention card moving to churned x. That one status change is what fires the rest.

Before you accept the cancel, offer to move the money

If they are under contract and want out, we do not pause and we do not shrink the invoice. We move the money. The same spend goes into SEO, or into whatever is actually going to work for them, and the contract stays whole.

A client who says "this is not working" is usually right about the channel and wrong about the agency. Moving budget is a real answer. Pausing is just a slower cancel.

Moving the money is a save, not a concession

It keeps the revenue, keeps the relationship, and gets them a result they can see. Offer it before you ever start the offboarding below.

How the offer runs

  1. It goes out in writing with the cancellation confirmation. Days 1 to 3. Not a phone mention, not a maybe. All three doors, with the prices.
  2. It carries a date. The site comes down 30 days after their last paid day unless they take one of the first two. Put the actual date in the email.
  3. Retention makes the offer, on the final call and in the email. While there is still goodwill. Not sales, this is not a new deal, it is keeping a door open.
  4. If they say yes, a new agreement gets signed and Victor sets up the new billing. File it through the Billing Change Request form so it does not live in somebody's head.
  5. If they say no or say nothing, the site comes down on the date in the email. We do not host for free and we do not quietly keep paying for someone who left.
No answer is not a yes

Without a date in the email, everybody picks ignore and we host their site for the rest of time. The date is the whole mechanism. If it is not in the email, the offer does not exist.

Telling someone is not a process. If a free month, a pause or a cancel only exists in a conversation, the client gets billed anyway and we find out when they call angry. From today there is one queue, it belongs to Victor Lemus, and nothing reaches it by mouth.

Victor Lemus owns the billing queue. He is the one who does or does not run the charge. Every billing hold and every refund goes to him. Do not go around him, and do not tell him in person. The task is how he finds out.

Everything else uses the form

pause billing change amount change billing date payment plan free month promised at signup

The form is a tab called BILLING FORM at the top of the retention list, next to List and Board. You do not leave ClickUp and you do not go hunting. It takes about twenty seconds: client, amount, the date it has to happen by, whether Christian already approved it, and one line saying what was promised and to who.

Nothing is done until the box is ticked

Every billing task carries a checkbox called BILLING CONFIRMED DONE. Whoever actually pulls the charge in GHL or PayBright ticks it. Not the person who asked. Not the manager.

An untick is not a small thing. It means the card is still live and the client is still going to get charged. If it is not ticked, it did not happen.

Website clients are the dangerous ones

Almost everything we bill is charged by hand. For those clients a free month or a cancel just means Victor does not run the charge. Nothing else has to happen.

Websites are automatic. That subscription bills itself whether anyone is paying attention or not. A website client who is given a free month, paused or cancelled will still be charged unless the recurring billing is actually stopped in the system. Say the word "website" out loud when you file one of these.

What ClickUp cannot do

ClickUp cannot reach into GHL or PayBright and stop a charge. No software here does that. What the automation guarantees is that the request exists, has an owner, has a due date, and cannot be quietly dropped. Victor Lemus still pulls the card.
