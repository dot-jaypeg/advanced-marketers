The Read on This Week

Meta spend scaled 50 percent week over week and cost per lead held essentially flat — $30.09 against $29.84 the prior week, a 0.9 percent move. That is the number that matters: the account absorbed half again as much budget without the auction pushing lead costs up, which means there is still headroom above the current daily rate.

Lead volume grew 48.8 percent, tracking spend almost exactly. Impressions grew 38.2 percent while link clicks grew only 19.7 percent, so click-through softened from 1.48 to 1.29 percent even as the lead rate per click improved from 6.21 to 7.72 percent. Traffic got slightly less efficient at the top and noticeably more efficient at the bottom.

$44,269 in cash was collected against $3,671 in measured media spend. That ratio is not an attributed return — nearly all of it is existing-client retainer billing, and with the pipeline connector down there is no way to tie any dollar to any ad this week. Treat it as a cash-flow figure, not a ROAS figure.

Method — What Was Not Done

The qualified-lead sample check (up to 15 contacts reviewed through conversation history to reclassify spam, solicitors and wrong-service enquiries) was not run, because the conversations scope returns 401. Zero contacts were checked and zero were reclassified. Every lead figure in this report is therefore a raw platform-reported count with no human-equivalent verification applied — the true qualified number is lower than 122 by an unmeasured margin.

Channel attribution rules (growth-diagnostic tag to ChatGPT, facebook UTM or fbc click ID to Meta, gclid to Google, affiliate and partner-name tags to Affiliate, remainder to Organic/Other) could not be applied to any lead, because contact tags and attributions are unreadable without the contacts scope.

Cash ROAS and LTV-based ROAS are deliberately omitted per channel. With no attribution layer, dividing $44,269 of mostly-recurring retainer cash by $3,671 of Meta spend would produce a 12x figure that means nothing. For reference, the LTV model stands unchanged: LTV equals monthly retainer divided by 0.15, so a $2,500 per month client is worth approximately $16,667. The median retainer collected this week was $2,500.
