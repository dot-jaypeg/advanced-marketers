3 · What Kyle did well — worth repeating

  • Speed to lead was excellent. Form at 11:33, first text at 12:49, live on the phone at 12:55. Roughly 80 minutes from stranger to conversation.
  • Honest positioning, no inflation. "We really only do this for contractors" — no Premier Partner boast, no $2.4M ad-spend line, no ten-years claim. Cleanest opening we've reviewed.
  • Recovered a broken calendar link himself rather than letting the meeting die — texted and emailed the Zoom room within five minutes of Edwin flagging it.
  • Verified the email twice, and caught a real problem. On the close call he noticed the contract email wasn't Edwin's working inbox and added a personal one, spelling it back letter by letter [02:16–03:00]. That is precisely the failure that stalled New Generation Construction and Sunset Walls — Atlas invites landing in a dead mailbox. He prevented it without being asked.
  • The client is genuinely enthusiastic. He signed unprompted and said so: "I'm happy to say we will be going with you guys." This is a warm, willing account.

4 · What's actually at risk

  1. We sold a business we know almost nothing about. Across 7 minutes of recorded conversation we never asked ticket size, annual revenue, margin, crew capacity, service area, budget, or who decides. What we know came from Edwin volunteering it: no real marketing history, referral-driven, "pretty much built up old school way," has a website he thinks is decorative, BBB-affiliated. That is not enough to build a campaign on — and the call that was supposed to gather it is still scheduled for Friday.
  2. The ad-budget conversation has not happened. He runs zero ads today. When the growth specialist asks him to fund Google or Meta on top of $2,500, that will land as a surprise unless someone gets ahead of it this week. Two accounts have already cancelled or gone red over precisely this.
  3. He has no go-live date. His only expectation is a text "by Friday or Monday." Day 14 is September 30 and nobody has told him.
  4. The calendar link is broken and it nearly cost us the deal. "On my end it shows as in (event cancel)." This is an ops bug, not a sales one, and it is almost certainly hitting other booked leads silently. Worth checking today.
  5. Process question for you, not a rep issue: contract sent 39 minutes after a no-show, discovery pushed to after the close. Kyle read a warm buyer correctly and it worked. But if this becomes the pattern, we are systematically selling before we qualify — and delivery inherits accounts nobody scoped.
