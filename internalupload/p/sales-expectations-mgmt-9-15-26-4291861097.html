The standard everything below is built on
We go live on day 14. Every account. Whether or not the client gets us everything.

Part 1

Team-facing findings & the required fix

No names. Safe to share with the sales team.

1 · What onboarding is reporting

From the 09/02 Client Updates meeting and the onboarding team's written notes, the same complaint is recurring across accounts:

"Sales team is not providing the complete information to our clients… when they see that we need information from them to fill out Atlas and creating GBP, meta or Google ADS they always say 'I've been told by my sales rep that you'll be handling EVERYTHING.'" — Onboarding team, Client Updates notes, 09/02/2026
"I think there's… a miscommunication between our clients and sales. Because it seems that sales is telling them that we're going to do everything, and they don't need to do or fill out Atlas or to complete something." — Onboarding lead, recorded internal call, 09/02/2026 [00:32:26–00:32:50]

Accounts showing this pattern: Sunset Walls & Ceilings · Viper Rooter & Plumbing · Green Lawn Care · New Generation Construction · O Painting. A sixth, Bracero Plumbing, was flagged separately by the growth team over the cost of ad spend.

2 · What the recorded sales call actually shows

We pulled the full transcript of a 56-minute discovery and pitch call behind one of these accounts — a $2,500/month deal closed in late August. Three findings.

FINDING 1 — Pricing and ad spend were disclosed properly. This part is clean.
The fee, the step-up, the contract term and the fact that ad budget is separate and additional were all stated — twice, including a direct answer when the client asked about extra costs.
36:50"This is our service charge. You still have to allocate some money for your ad spend itself. Normally, when people come on, they'll do anywhere between 500 and $1,000 for the first two months."
41:03"Your card is hooked up to Google and it's hooked up to Meta. We don't have our fingers in that transaction at all."

Fee terms were stated plainly too: $2,500/mo for the first two months, $3,500/mo from month three, six-month agreement with the first two months cancellable. Nobody hid a fee. The one thing to tighten is the $500–$1,000 ad-spend anchor — when the real recommended budget lands above it, the client experiences the difference as a bait. Give a realistic range, not a floor.

FINDING 2 — Client access was never asked for. This is the root cause.
We searched the full transcript: zero mentions of Atlas. No mention of needing a Meta Business account, Google Business Profile access, a domain, photos or a license. The only client effort described was a single onboarding Zoom.
38:17"In about two days, you're gonna get on board, get on a Zoom call like this with the onboarding person, and they're gonna start getting information, all the information that we need from you."

Read this part carefully, because it matters: the rep never said "we handle everything." Every instance of the word "everything" in that call came from the client, not from us. What happened instead is that the pitch listed roughly ten things Advanced Marketers does — website, landing pages, service and city pages, 10 creatives a month, CRM, automations, review engine, dedicated growth specialist — and zero things the client does. A reasonable buyer walks away believing we're handling everything without anyone having lied to him.

That makes this a script problem, not an honesty problem — and it's fixable in one paragraph.

FINDING 3 — The timeline was wrong and the results promises were absolute.
Two different problems sit in this list, and they need opposite fixes.
38:39"Your first ad will be live within a week of coming on board with us."
38:07"It's gonna start getting those residential calls, like, within a week."
44:39"Drywall stuff is easy. We can get you drywall work next week, literally."
46:45"This is for sure going to work for your business. This is for sure going to get you more residential jobs."
47:02"Within the first month of you paying for it… it will have paid for itself for sure."
The go-live promiseThe results promises
Wrong number, right instinct. "Live within a week" isn't our standard — day 14 is. Don't soften it, correct it. Say 14 days, say it more than once, and mean it. This is a date we control. Reframe these. "Paid for itself in the first month" is a revenue promise, and we don't control their closing. Commit to leads in the first month, of the type they described — then be straight that turning those into signed jobs depends on their sales cycle.

Reality check: that account was sold 8/28 and still wasn't live at day 14 on 9/11, and 11 accounts are currently past the 14-day SLA. In almost every case we missed because we sat waiting on the client instead of launching around them. That is exactly what the fallbacks below exist to end.

3 · The fix — a mandatory close-out on every sales call

Sixty seconds before you take payment. Say it out loud, in this order, every time. It costs you nothing on the close and it removes the single biggest cause of week-two cancellations.

1What we do
"Here's everything on our side: website build, landing pages, ad campaigns on Google and Meta, ten creatives a month, your CRM, review engine, and a growth specialist you'll talk to weekly."
2The 14-day standard — say it here, first
"Our standard is that you go live in 14 days. Not a target we hope to hit — that's the standard, and we hit it whether or not every piece comes back from you."
3What we need from you — and why it's worth doing fast
"To make that launch as strong as possible I need a few things from you: Atlas, that's our onboarding form — about 30 to 45 minutes and we'll push through it with you. Access to your Google Business Profile. A Meta Business account, and we'll set one up with you if you don't have one. Your domain. Photos of your work and your license number. The faster that comes back, the better this performs — it's your brand and your jobs in the ads instead of ours."
4What happens if it doesn't come back — we launch anyway
"And if you can't get me some of it, we don't sit and wait. We launch on day 14 regardless — here's exactly how."

The fallbacks — know these cold, say them by name

If we don't get…We still go live on day 14 by…
A domainBuying and registering one ourselves so nothing stalls. We hold it while you're with us and transfer it to you when the engagement ends. Say the transfer part out loud — don't let them find out later.
Photos / creative assetsRunning our own proven creatives from campaigns we've already run in your trade. We know they convert. Yours will convert better once we have them.
Meta accessLaunching on Google instead and putting the budget there.
Google accessLaunching on Meta instead and putting the budget there.
Any ad platform accessLaunching SEO and holding there. The moment access lands we switch the paid side on. If it's still not there after about a month, your growth specialist resets the plan with you — we don't leave you parked.
Website content or copyBuilding it from your trade, your service area and what you told us on this call. You review and we adjust.

Then close the loop on cost and on leads

5What it costs — fee and ad budget separately
"$2,500 a month for the first two months, then $3,500 from month three, on a six-month agreement. Separate from that, you pay Google and Meta directly for your ad budget — that's your card, not ours. For your market I'd budget roughly $X–$Y a month to get real volume. So plan on about $Z all-in."
6Say day 14 again — then set the lead expectation honestly
"So: onboarding call within two business days, Atlas in the first week, and you're live on day 14. Once we're live, you will start getting leads inside that first month — and they'll be the jobs you just described to me, [kitchen and bath remodels, drywall], not random noise. What I won't pretend is that every one of them closes the week it comes in. Depending on your sales cycle that's usually another week or two. So judge us on the leads first, and the revenue right behind them."

The line to hold: commit hard to what we control — the launch date, the deliverables, the fallbacks, and the fact that leads start coming in once we're live. Don't commit to what we don't control — how fast those leads turn into signed revenue. That depends on their sales cycle and their closing, and saying otherwise is what turns a normal ramp into a refund demand.

4 · Onboarding says the same thing, in the same words

This only works if the client hears it twice. If sales says day 14 and onboarding opens with "we'll get started once you send everything over," we're back where we started. Onboarding's opening 45 seconds:

1Set the clock immediately
"Before anything else — our mission is to have you live in 14 days, and we're going to hit that. You're on day [X] right now."
2Frame Atlas honestly and move fast
"This is Atlas, our onboarding form. It's about 30 to 45 minutes and I'm going to move us through it quickly — I'll fill in everything I can from your sales call so you're not repeating yourself."
3Ask for access, and surface blockers now
"I need Google Business Profile access, a Meta Business account, your domain, photos and your license. If there's anything you can't get me, tell me right now rather than later — we have a workaround for every one of them and I'd rather use it than lose days."
4Repeat the standard on the way out
"Either way you're going live on day 14. Access just decides how strong we come out of the gate."

6 · How this gets checked

The weekly call-QA program will score the close-out as a pass/fail item on every closed deal, under Pitch & value framing and Compliance & professionalism. Five checks:

  1. Was "live on day 14" said — and said more than once?
  2. Were the fallbacks named? Domain, our creatives, platform switch, SEO holding pattern.
  3. Were client deliverables named specifically — Atlas at 30–45 minutes, GBP, Meta, domain, photos, license?
  4. Was ad spend stated as separate and additional, with a realistic range?
  5. Was the lead expectation set as leads in the first month, of the type the client described — with the sales-cycle caveat? Go-live and lead-flow commitments are expected. Revenue, ROI and "pays for itself" are not.

All sales calls are recorded and QA-reviewed; the team is already aware of this program. Nothing here changes that.

The 14-day commitment cuts both ways

Once reps are saying "you go live on day 14 no matter what" on every call — and repeating it — that stops being an aspiration and becomes a promise the client will hold us to. 11 accounts are past SLA right now. The fallbacks are what make the promise deliverable, so they have to be real and staffed: someone has to actually be authorised to buy a domain, pull house creatives, and flip a launch from Meta to Google or to SEO without waiting for a decision. Worth confirming with Jonathan that delivery can carry it before sales starts saying it.

Recommended actions

  1. Coach Kyle on the "stated hesitation" pattern — when a prospect says they need to consult a partner, book the follow-up rather than re-closing. Second instance in three weeks.
  2. Roll the close-out and the fallbacks in a single sales meeting, with onboarding in the room so both sides hear the same wording at the same time.
  3. Absolute revenue promises are now a scored item for the whole team, not just Kyle. He is the one we have a transcript for, not necessarily the only one doing it.
  4. Decide whether Sunset Walls is savable — part-time operator, never understood his obligations, past SLA. Either launch him on fallbacks this week or offer an adapted plan.
  5. Fix the name on the Sunset Walls records before any further billing goes out.
