Team-facing findings & the required fix

No names. Safe to share with the sales team.

1 · What onboarding is reporting

From the 09/02 Client Updates meeting and the onboarding team's written notes, the same complaint is recurring across accounts:

"Sales team is not providing the complete information to our clients… when they see that we need information from them to fill out Atlas and creating GBP, meta or Google ADS they always say 'I've been told by my sales rep that you'll be handling EVERYTHING.'" — Onboarding team, Client Updates notes, 09/02/2026
"I think there's… a miscommunication between our clients and sales. Because it seems that sales is telling them that we're going to do everything, and they don't need to do or fill out Atlas or to complete something." — Onboarding lead, recorded internal call, 09/02/2026 [00:32:26–00:32:50]

Accounts showing this pattern: Sunset Walls & Ceilings · Viper Rooter & Plumbing · Green Lawn Care · New Generation Construction · O Painting. A sixth, Bracero Plumbing, was flagged separately by the growth team on the cost of ad spend.

2 · What the recorded sales call actually shows

We pulled the full transcript of the 56-minute discovery/pitch call behind one of these accounts — a $2,500/month deal closed in late August. Three findings.

FINDING 1 — Pricing and ad spend were disclosed properly. This part is clean.
The rep stated the fee, the step-up, the contract term, and the fact that ad budget is separate and additional — twice, including a direct answer when the client asked about extra costs.
36:50"This is our service charge. You still have to allocate some money for your ad spend itself. Normally, when people come on, they'll do anywhere between 500 and $1,000 for the first two months."
41:03"Your card is hooked up to Google and it's hooked up to Meta. We don't have our fingers in that transaction at all."

Fee terms were also stated plainly: $2,500/mo for the first two months, $3,500/mo from month three, six-month agreement with the first two months cancellable. Nobody hid a fee. The residual friction is the $500–$1,000 ad-spend anchor — when the real recommended budget lands above it, the client experiences the difference as a surprise. Give a realistic range, not a floor.

FINDING 2 — Client responsibilities were never stated. This is the root cause.
Searched the full transcript: zero mentions of Atlas. No mention of needing a Meta Business account, Google Business Profile access, photos, assets, or a license. The only client effort described was a single onboarding Zoom.
38:17"In about two days, you're gonna get on board, get on a Zoom call like this with the onboarding person, and they're gonna start getting information, all the information that we need from you."

Read this part carefully, because it matters: the rep never said "we handle everything." Every instance of the word "everything" in the call came from the client, not the rep. What happened instead is that the pitch listed roughly ten things Advanced Marketers does — website, landing pages, service and city pages, 10 creatives a month, CRM, automations, review engine, dedicated growth specialist — and zero things the client does. A reasonable buyer concludes we're handling everything without anyone having lied to him.

That makes this a script defect, not a dishonesty problem — and it's fixable in one paragraph.

FINDING 3 — Timeline and outcome language we cannot deliver on.
This is the real exposure, and it is the part that has to stop.
38:07"It's gonna start getting those residential calls, like, within a week."
38:39"Your first ad will be live within a week of coming on board with us."
44:39"Drywall stuff is easy. We can get you drywall work next week, literally."
46:45"This is for sure going to work for your business. This is for sure going to get you more residential jobs."
47:02"Within the first month of you paying for it… it will have paid for itself for sure."

Reality check: this account was sold on 8/28 and was still not live at day 14 on 9/11. Across the book, 11 accounts are currently past the 14-day go-live SLA. "For sure" appears three times in ninety seconds. The word "guarantee" is never used — that distinction will matter to a lawyer and to nobody else.

3 · The fix — a mandatory close-out on every sales call

Sixty seconds before you take payment. Say it out loud, in this order, every time. It costs you nothing on the close and it removes the single biggest cause of week-two cancellations.

1What we do
"Here's everything on our side: website build, landing pages, ad campaigns on Google and Meta, ten creatives a month, your CRM, review engine, and a growth specialist you'll talk to weekly."
2What we need from you — name every item
"Here's what we'll need from you, and I want to be upfront because it's real work: an onboarding form called Atlas that takes about 30–45 minutes, access to your Google Business Profile, a Meta Business account — we can help you set one up if you don't have one — plus photos of your work and your license number. If we don't get those, we can't launch. Are you able to get me that in the first week?"
3What it costs — fee and budget separately
"$2,500 a month for the first two months, then $3,500 from month three, on a six-month agreement. Separate from that, you'll pay Google and Meta directly for your ad budget — that's your card, not ours. For your market I'd budget roughly $X–$Y a month to get real volume. So plan on about $Z all-in."
4When things actually happen — no "for sure"
"Onboarding call within two business days. Website mockups for your review in the first week. We target going live by day 14, and that clock depends on how fast we get your assets. Paid ads usually need 30–60 days of data before performance settles — I'm not going to tell you the phone rings next week, because that's not how this works."

4 · Language to retire · language to use

Stop sayingSay instead
"Your first ad will be live within a week.""We target day 14, and it depends on how fast we get your assets."
"This is for sure going to work.""Here's what we've done for similar contractors. Here's what I think is realistic for you."
"It will have paid for itself in the first month, for sure.""Most accounts need 30–60 days before the numbers stabilize. I'd judge us at 90 days."
"We can get you work next week, literally.""Leads typically start coming in once ads have been live a couple of weeks."
Listing only what we do.Listing what we do and what you'll need from them, in the same breath.
"Ad spend is usually $500–$1,000."A realistic range for that market, framed as a floor and a recommended level.

5 · How this gets checked

The weekly call-QA program will score the close-out block as a pass/fail item on every closed deal, under Pitch & value framing and Compliance & professionalism. Four checks:

  1. Were client deliverables named specifically — Atlas, GBP, Meta, assets?
  2. Was ad spend stated as separate and additional, with a realistic range?
  3. Was the go-live timeline stated as day 14 and conditional on assets?
  4. Were there any absolute outcome promises — "for sure", "guaranteed", "paid for itself"?

All sales calls are recorded and QA-reviewed; the team is already aware of this program. Nothing here changes that.

Two other things worth Paul knowing

  • The "Eduardo Martinez" record error starts here. Both partners were on the call and Kyle addressed follow-ups to Eduardo. The opportunity, the $2,500 invoice and the signed agreement all went out in the wrong partner's name against contact "Aldo Martinez."   • Affordability. Our own intake fields record projected revenue of $100K–$250K and "no marketing experience." $2,500 rising to $3,500 plus ad spend is roughly 15–40% of projected revenue. Worth a qualification gate, not a compliance finding.
  • Headcount inconsistency [33:28]: "we're, like, 25 people, a little bit less than 25 people." Same wobble noted on 08/28. Give the team one number.

Recommended actions

  1. Coach Kyle on the "stated hesitation" pattern — when a prospect says they need to consult a partner, book the follow-up rather than re-closing. Second instance in three weeks.
  2. Absolute-outcome language is now a scored item for the whole team, not just Kyle. He is the one we have a transcript for, not necessarily the only one doing it.
  3. Decide whether Sunset Walls is savable — the client is a part-time operator who never understood his own obligations and is past SLA. Either assign someone to walk him through Atlas personally or offer an adapted plan.
  4. Fix the name on the Sunset Walls records before any further billing goes out.
